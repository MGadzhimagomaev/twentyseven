20/7

Privacy

Last updated 24 November 2026

20/7 is a small personal project for counting what you train each week. This page says what it stores, why, and how to get it back or delete it.

What 20/7 stores

  • Your email address, to sign you in with a code. There are no passwords.
  • Your settings: weekly goal, the day weeks start, daily step goal, time zone.
  • Your groups and workouts, and every set or workout you log, with its date and time.
  • A log of changes, such as a new goal or a renamed group, so past weeks keep the targets they had.
  • If you use the iPhone app and allow it: your daily step totals from Apple Health.
  • Feedback you send from the app, with your email address, so the developer can reply. It is also emailed to the developer through Resend.

What it doesn't do

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling or sharing of data.
  • No outside fonts or scripts: everything loads from this site, except the database itself.

Where it lives

  • In a database run by Supabase in the EU (Ireland), which also handles sign-in.
  • Sign-in emails go out through an email-sending service.
  • On your own device, 20/7 keeps a copy of your recent data and any changes not saved yet, so it works without a connection. Signing out removes it.

Who can see it

Only you, through your account: the database lets each person read and change only their own data. The developer can reach the database to run and fix the service, and doesn't look at anyone's data for any other reason.

Steps from Apple Health

The iPhone app only reads step counts, only after you allow it, and only to show your own steps and averages. Health data is never used for ads or marketing, never sold, and not stored in iCloud.

Your choices

  • Export: Settings › Your data gives every set as a spreadsheet, or everything as a file.
  • Delete: Settings › Delete account removes your account and all its data right away.
  • You can also ask what is stored about you, or ask for it to be corrected or deleted.

Contact

Ask the person who invited you to the test, or write through the project's GitHub page.
